## Expanded Faculties of the Stoneborn Institute
Adding **more balance**, especially focusing on arts, diplomacy, and cultural preservation.

#### 1. Faculty of the Forge (Craftsmanship & Magic)
(_Previously focused on blacksmithing and metallurgy—now expanded to cover textiles, artistry, and fine craftsmanship._)

- **Runesmithing & Enchantment** – Traditional, but open to all. Many dwarven women are **Runescribes**, responsible for carving protective sigils into armour, architecture, and heirlooms.
- **Advanced Metallurgy** – Crafting more than just weapons—**fine silverwork, coronets, and intricate decorative armour**.
- **Jewellery & Gemcutting** – A revered craft. Matriarchs often **design ceremonial pieces** for lineage marking.
- **Clothcraft & Tapestry Weaving** – Magic-infused textiles, banners that tell clan histories, and warded cloaks.
- **Sacred Craft of Hearth & Home** – Creating family heirlooms, ritual items, and traditional dwarven furniture.

🪶 _New Method of Learning: Apprentices travel deep into the mines to retrieve rare materials and must weave their personal history into their first tapestry or jewellery piece._

---

#### 2. Faculty of Stone & Earth (Mining, Architecture & Hearthcraft)
(_Previously focused on engineering—now expanded to include home-building, medicine, and subterranean agriculture._)

- **Mining Engineering** – Still a major focus, but with the addition of **designing safe underground homes, public spaces, and sanctuaries**.
- **Grand Halls & Hearthfire** – Designing the heart of a dwarven settlement: feasting halls, family hearths, and **sacred places for storytelling**.
- **Subterranean Agriculture & Brewing** – Farming underground foods, managing sustainable food sources, and brewing **healing tonics & battle draughts**.
- **Healing Arts & Stonewarding** – A mix of **dwarven herbalism and magical warding**, ensuring dwarves remain strong in battle and in childbirth.

🛖 _New Method of Learning: Students must design and **construct a fully functional dwarven home**, blessed with protective enchantments, before graduating._

---

#### 3. Faculty of Lore & Lineage (History, Song & Cultural Arts)
(_Dwarves value history, but often forget the softer side of cultural preservation—this faculty teaches it all._)

- **The Great Songkeepers** – Dwarves don’t just **record history, they sing it**. Students must train in oral tradition, poetry, and crafting new sagas.
- **Lineage Studies & Family Archives** – Maintaining clan records, settling disputes over ancestry, and ensuring **lost lineages are restored**.
- **Runic Calligraphy & Decorative Inscription** – Ancient dwarven writing is an **art form**, often used for ceremonial purposes.
- **The Art of Hospitality & Hearthkeeping** – Diplomacy, event planning, and **hosting massive clan gatherings**.
- **Cultural Studies of the Surface & Underfolk** – Understanding elves, humans, and even goblins through their art, music, and stories.

🎶 _New Method of Learning: To graduate, a student must compose an epic about their own trials, which is then judged by the Matron Skalds._

---

#### 4. Faculty of War & Strategy (Combat, Leadership & Guardianship)
(_Dwarven warfare isn’t just brute strength—it’s about defending **homes, families, and traditions**._)

- **Guardianship & Shieldwall Tactics** – Training in **defensive battle formations**, ideal for protecting dwarven halls.
- **Tactics of the Clan Matrons** – Female dwarves have long played roles in **logistics, command, and supply lines**, crucial for prolonged sieges.
- **Oathbinding & Honour Pacts** – How to make, negotiate, and **enforce dwarven oaths**. A **woman’s word in a clan is often law**.
- **Battlefield Healing & Emergency Aid** – Not every warrior swings an axe—some wield bandages and poultices.
- **Defending Hearth & Hall** – Many dwarves fight **not on battlefields, but in their homes**—learning how to **fortify family spaces and protect their kin**.

🛡️ _New Method of Learning: Before earning a leadership title, students must oversee the defence of a small dwarven village under siege conditions in a live-training exercise._

---

#### 5. Faculty of Trade, Diplomacy & Guild Management
(_Focused on **practical leadership, negotiation, and wealth-building**—often led by dwarven matriarchs._)

- **Underworld Diplomacy** – Navigating **inter-clan disputes**, trade with elves, and **resolving grudges without war**.
- **Guild Mistress Training & Business Leadership** – Running forges, breweries, and **mercantile operations** that sustain dwarven strongholds.
- **Treasure Appraisal & Hoard Keeping** – Evaluating the worth of **artifacts, gold, and enchanted objects**.
- **Marriage Pacts & Political Alliances** – The art of securing **power through family bonds**—a role **heavily influenced by dwarven women**.
- **Sacred Oaths & Lawkeeping** – Overseeing **legal disputes, honour duels, and heritage claims**.

🪙 _New Method of Learning: Every student must complete a diplomatic mission—whether it be securing a **trade deal with elves**, resolving a **grudge between clans**, or overseeing **a contested treasure claim**._

## 1. The referral

On 12 November 1052, during a supervised medication round, the candidate recorded on the drug chart that a dose had been administered to a patient when it had not. The mentor identified the discrepancy within the hour on checking the stock count. The dose was then administered under supervision; no harm to the patient resulted. The candidate, when asked, said that he had "got ahead of himself" and had signed the chart in anticipation of giving the dose, intending to give it immediately afterwards, and had then been called away.

## 2. Initial assessment

In my assessment the referral is properly made. Recording an administration that has not occurred is a documentation error of the kind the profession treats as serious. It is not, on the account given, dishonest: the candidate did not conceal the error and gave a consistent account.

## 3. Meeting with the candidate, 21 November 1052

The candidate was visibly distressed. He repeated the account above without variation. He said the ward had been short-staffed and that he had been trying to be useful. He asked whether he would be removed from the programme. I explained the procedure.

## 4. Mentor's statement

Received 25 November. Consistent with the above. Recommends additional supervised medication practice before return; does not recommend removal.

## 5. Preliminary view

Documentation error without dishonesty. Warning; supervised package; reflective account. Not removal. Panel to be convened January.

N. Memoryvault

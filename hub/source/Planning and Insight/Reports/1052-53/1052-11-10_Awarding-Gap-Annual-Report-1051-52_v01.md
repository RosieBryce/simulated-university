# Awarding Gap Annual Report 1051-52

## Faculty of Health and Education

Planning and Insight. Version 01, 10 November 1052. Prepared for Council (C/1052/22, appendix) and the Faculty. Internal.

## How to read this report

- **Good honours** means a First or Upper Second class degree.
- **The awarding gap** is the good honours rate for Elf graduates minus the rate for Dwarf graduates, in percentage points. A positive figure means Elf graduates were awarded good honours more often.
- Graduating year **1052** means students who completed in academic year 1051-52. Figures for 1052 graduates are the most recent available; 1053 graduates will not be known until the summer exam boards.
- The Faculty's graduates in any one year number a few hundred. Year-on-year movements of several percentage points are within the range the numbers would produce by chance. Read the five-year table, not the latest point.
- Programme-level figures are suppressed where a group has fewer than 20 graduates.

## What sits behind the gap

The gap is not explained by species as such. Our analysis, consistent with the institutional picture, finds that it is carried by differences in the circumstances students arrive with:

- **Prior education.** Dwarf entrants are more likely to come through the vocational and apprenticeship routes than the academic route, and prior route is associated with first-year marks across all programmes.
- **Socioeconomic position.** The Faculty recruits more Dwarf students from the lower socioeconomic ranks. Socioeconomic rank is associated with first-generation status, with recorded disability, and with engagement in the first semester.
- **Disability.** Recorded disability rates differ by clan, and students with a recorded disability show lower average marks in the first year, narrowing by the third.
- **Engagement.** First-semester attendance and VLE engagement predict year outcome more strongly than any demographic variable. The gap in engagement between groups is small but it compounds.

When these are taken into account the residual difference by species is small. That is not the same as saying there is no problem; it says where the problem is. Interventions that act on engagement and on support in the first year are the ones the evidence supports.

## Programme level

Programme-level figures are available in the extract for programmes with 20 or more graduates in a group. Every programme in the Faculty graduates enough students to report overall; split by species, a few fall below the threshold in one group and are suppressed. Programmes with smaller cohorts move by several points from year to year on their own. The Faculty should be cautious about any programme-level claim, and should not set programme-level targets.

## Recommendations

1. Report the five-year table, not the single-year figure.
2. Target first-year engagement and first-semester support, where the evidence is.
3. Do not set a target expressed as a single-year gap figure; the number will move by several points for reasons unrelated to any action.
4. Ask Planning and Insight before quoting a programme-level figure.

## Prompt

### What we're building

A new, self-contained feature in the `simulated-university` repo: a **simulated institutional SharePoint estate** for Stonegrove University, downloadable as a whole or in parts, with a browsable SharePoint-style front end on the existing GitHub Pages site. It is a safe sandbox where learners (the same audience as the Stonegrove dataset: HE professional services and academic staff, students, hackathon participants) can practise using AI on realistic, messy institutional documents with no real data at risk.

This is a new growth on the same branch: same universe, same lore, same clans, same public site, same repo. Before touching anything, read `CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`, `docs/DESIGN.md`, and `curriculum-and-lore/World-building/` (especially `Universities/Stonegrove University.md`, `clan summaries.md`, `Dwarven Pedagogy.md`). Programme and faculty names come from `config/programme_characteristics.csv`. Character names come from `config/clan_name_pools.yaml` and `curriculum-and-lore/name patterns.xlsx`. Do not invent a parallel universe.

In-world the thing is called **Stonegrove Hub**. The old network drive it replaced was called **StoneShare**, and the migration from one to the other is a storyline. Nothing inside the downloadable folder tree refers to SharePoint, Microsoft, exercises, learners, prompts or AI skills. All fourth-wall material lives on the website.

### The skills it exists to teach

Every design choice serves one or more of these. Each needs a place in the estate where the problem is sitting, waiting to be found.

**Core**

1. **Meeting transcripts to minutes and actions.** Long, messy auto-transcripts (Teams-style: timestamps, speaker names, cross-talk, mis-transcriptions of elvish and dwarvish names, tangents, someone's dog). Learners turn them into minutes in the house style, extract actions with owners and dates, and reconcile against a stale actions log.
2. **Data analysis.** Already covered by the Stonegrove dataset. The estate needs to link to it convincingly: a Planning & Insight team with extracts, requests, half-finished analyses, and a note pointing at the data source, so that the dataset becomes "the numbers this team is sitting on".
3. **Project management, seeding a bespoke app.** A team drowning in a process run on a monstrous spreadsheet, emails and good intentions. Everything needed to scope a small tool is lying around in pieces: requirements disguised as complaints, a workflow nobody has written down, a project request form from Digital Services, a business case template, a previous request that was turned down. The learner should arrive at "we could build something for this" without being told.
4. **Folder organisation: imposing structure and rules.** A library that is chaos (the StoneShare migration dump) alongside a naming convention and retention schedule that exist and were never followed. Learners work out what should be there, write rules, de-duplicate, decide what is current, and propose a structure.
5. **AI guardrails.** Sensitive material is scattered through the estate: some correctly labelled Confidential, some unlabelled, some in the wrong place. Learners practise instructing an AI to work across a folder without touching Confidential material, then check whether it did. The estate must make that checkable.

**Additional** (each needs its own seeded material; keep them light)

6. **Briefing a busy reader.** Turn a 40-page committee pack or a sprawling report into a one-page brief for the Dean, keeping what matters and flagging what is contested. Seed: Council papers, a long external review, a Dean who has written "can someone tell me what this actually says" on the covering email.
7. **Extracting structure from unstructured documents.** Thirty placement-provider letters and emails, each with a contact, capacity, and conditions buried in prose; the learner produces one clean table. Seed: the Programme Support Team's `Provider correspondence` folder.
8. **Checking the AI's work.** A document where someone has already used AI (a summary, a set of minutes, a translated policy) and it contains errors: a wrong date, an invented action, a figure that doesn't match the source. The learner's job is verification, not generation. Seed: a few files with a telltale "Summary (auto-generated)" header and the source they claim to summarise.
9. **Reconciling versions.** Five files called FINAL. Which is current, what changed between them, and which changes were deliberate. Seed: the placement spreadsheet lineage and a policy with three competing drafts.
10. **Translating institutional voice.** Rewrite a dense regulation as student-facing guidance, or draft a difficult email (to a placement provider, to an applicant, to a colleague who sent the Confidential file to the wrong list) in the right register. Seed: regulations in Quality, the incident in Student Wellbeing, applicant comms in Admissions.
11. **Following brand guidelines consistently.** Give an AI the brand guide and ask it to produce or fix a run of material (an open day flyer, a programme leaflet, a set of social posts, a slide template, an email signature block) and check whether it holds the line on tone of voice, naming, colours, type, logo use and the things the guide says never to do. Then feed it the off-brand material already in the estate and ask it to bring that into line. Seed: the Communications site's brand guide and assets, plus off-brand documents scattered across other sites (a department that made its own logo, a leaflet in the wrong typeface, a programme that calls itself by a name the brand guide retired).

### The governance layer (in-world, standard-looking)

A Governance & Secretariat site holding documents that look exactly like what a UK university would have, written in that register:

- **AI Acceptable Use Policy**: permitted uses, prohibited uses, approved tools, disclosure, data protection cross-references, accountability sits with the human, review date. Owned by Governance & Secretariat, drafted with Digital Services (off-stage).
- **Data Classification Scheme**: three labels, **Public**, **Internal**, **Confidential**. One page. Examples of each. What you may and may not do with each, including: Confidential material must not be entered into any AI tool not on the approved list.
- **"Can I use AI for this?"**: one-page decision tree.
- **Approved tools list**: fictional in-world product names, short.
- **Information governance essentials**: retention schedule (simple), naming convention, records-of-processing stub, DPIA template.
- **Committee papers and minutes** in house style. These double as the exemplars for the minutes exercise.

The policies should be good enough to be used as the ruleset in the guardrails and folder-organisation exercises, and slightly dated or contradictory in realistic ways (the naming convention says one thing, half the estate does another; the AI policy post-dates most of the documents and nobody has caught up).

### Shape of the estate

One faculty, six professional-service areas, one governance site. Each is a SharePoint "site" with one or more document libraries.

| Site | Why it's here | Skills it mainly serves |
|---|---|---|
| **Faculty of Health & Education**: Dean's Office; Dept of Health; Dept of Education; the StoneShare migration library | The faculty. Placements and fitness to practise generate real sensitivity. The Dean's Office is where briefings land. | 1, 4, 5, 6 |
| **Programme Support Team: Nursing and Care** | Runs placements for the biggest programme on one spreadsheet. The app seed and the provider-correspondence pile. | 3, 7, 9 |
| **Planning & Insight** | Owns the numbers. Extracts generated from the Stonegrove dataset. | 2, 6, 8 |
| **Quality** | Regulations, revalidation, exam board servicing, external examiner reports. Formal register. | 1, 9, 10 |
| **Admissions** | Applicant data (fabricated), offer-making minutes, conversion worries, open day planning, applicant comms. | 5, 10, 2 |
| **Student Wellbeing** | Support plans, case notes, DSA evidence. The Confidential heartland, and the near-miss incident. | 5, 4, 10 |
| **Communications** | Brand guide, brand assets, templates, campaign folders, a `Requests` library full of half-briefed jobs from other teams. | 11, 10 |
| **Governance & Secretariat** | Policies, Council and Senate papers, the classification scheme. | all |

**The Communications site** is the one place where hand-supplied files are expected. Structure it as `Brand` (brand guide, logo files, colour and type references, templates), `Campaigns` (open days, clearing, a programme launch), `Requests` (briefs from other teams, mostly bad) and `Archive` (the previous brand, still in use in places). The build ships a draft brand guide written in-world (tone of voice, naming rules including retired programme names, colour palette drawn from `docs/style.css`, type, logo clear space, a "never do this" page) and placeholder assets (the coat of arms from `curriculum-and-lore/World-building/assets/Stonegrove/`, simple generated colour swatches and a plain template), all clearly listed in `hub/bible/hand_supplied.yaml`. Anything listed there is treated as **hand-supplied**: the build copies it in if present and never regenerates or overwrites it, so Rosie can replace the placeholders at leisure. Off-brand material for skill 11 is planted in other sites and recorded in the bible with what is wrong about each.

**Size:** roughly **450 authored documents**, becoming **700 to 900 files** once the mess (copies, stale versions, litter, empty folders) is applied, under 100 MB total so it sits comfortably on Pages and downloads in one go. The StoneShare migration dump takes most of the derived volume; it should feel big. Phase 0 sets a per-site budget in `sites.yaml` so the total doesn't drift. Most files are small. Transcripts (3,000 to 6,000 words each) and spreadsheets are the heavyweights.

**Timeframe:** the estate is a snapshot taken in **spring 1053**, late in the final year the dataset covers, with documents going back five or six years and thinning out as they age.

### The cast

Keep a **cast bible** (out-of-world, in the source folder, never shipped): 25 to 35 named staff across the sites, each with species, clan, role, a line on how they write (terse; florid; all-caps subject lines; never uses the template), what they care about, and who they clash with. Names from the clan name pools. Use the clan cultural traits as light texture rather than caricature: a Slate dwarf in Planning who files everything perfectly, a Rowan elf in the Dean's office who has never once named a file correctly.

Characters recur. The same person appears in a transcript, as a document author, in an email thread, in a staff directory, and as the owner of the "Old stuff" folder that is the problem. Learners should start to recognise people.

**Contact details** are always safe pretend values. Email addresses use the domain `stonegrove.ac.example` (the `.example` TLD is reserved and can never resolve). Phone numbers come from the Ofcom drama ranges: landlines `01632 960000` to `01632 960999`, mobiles `07700 900000` to `07700 900999`. Internal extensions are four digits. No postcodes that could be real; use the in-world campus address. Put these rules in the bible and have the build lint for any other `@` domain or UK-looking number.

**Overarching storylines** that leave traces across several sites (pick five to seven, document them in the bible with which files carry each thread):

- The **Health & Education awarding gap**, the dataset's own headline, has reached the Dean, and Council want a paper. Planning & Insight have been asked; the extract is half done; the Dean's office has a draft with the wrong numbers in it.
- **Placement allocation** for Nursing and Care runs on one spreadsheet with 14 tabs, maintained by one person who is about to go on leave. This is the bespoke-app seed. A Digital Services project request was submitted two years ago and declined for lack of a business case.
- A **programme revalidation** (Occupational Therapy, say) grinding through Quality with papers, minutes, and a transcript of a panel meeting.
- The **new AI Acceptable Use Policy**, recently approved, contested in committee, not yet understood. A transcript where it is debated; emails asking "so can I or can't I".
- A **near-miss data incident**: a Confidential support plan was emailed to a whole programme team. Incident log, a terse email, a policy reminder, and the file still sitting where it shouldn't be.
- The **StoneShare migration**: the faculty's old network drive was lifted and dropped into one library with no curation. This is the folder-organisation exercise, and where most of the stray sensitive files live.
- An **NSS dip** in one department and the slightly panicked response.
- An **Admissions conversion scare**: offers up, acceptances down, and three competing explanations in three different documents.
- The **rebrand that didn't take**: a new brand guide two years ago, a department that kept its own logo, and Communications quietly losing the argument.

Storylines should be discoverable by exploring rather than announced. The website's hints can point learners at them.

### The mess (deliberate and documented)

The build must produce, on purpose and from declared rules so the site knows the truth:

- Version chaos: `Placement Allocation 1052-53 FINAL.xlsx`, `Placement Allocation 1052-53 FINAL v2.xlsx`, `Placement Allocation 1052-53 FINAL FINAL (use this one).xlsx`, `Copy of Placement Allocation 1052-53.xlsx`, `Placement Allocation (1).xlsx`, `Placement Allocation_old.xlsx`, `Placement Allocation_DRAFT_DO_NOT_CIRCULATE.xlsx`. Some "FINAL" files are older than their "DRAFT" siblings. Some duplicates are byte-identical; some differ in one paragraph.
- Dates in filenames in at least three formats; dates in metadata that disagree with the filename.
- Folders called `New folder`, `New folder (2)`, `Old`, `Archive - DO NOT DELETE`, `Misc`, `Hesta stuff`, `To sort`. Empty folders. Folders nested eight deep with one file at the bottom.
- Files in the wrong site entirely. A personal CV. Holiday photos (a handful, small). Zero-byte files. `.tmp` litter.
- Saved emails (`.eml`) and an `Email attachments` folder full of orphaned attachments with no context.
- Naming convention applied beautifully in one library and nowhere else.
- Metadata (`Modified`, `Modified By`, `Label`) populated inconsistently, as it would be.
- Sensitive content: some correctly labelled Confidential and in the right place; some labelled Internal when it should be Confidential; some unlabelled; at least one Confidential file sitting in a Public-labelled folder. All of it fabricated about fictional elves and dwarves, written with the right texture (a support plan reads like a support plan).
- A few files that are AI-generated in-world, with a "Summary (auto-generated)" header and planted errors, for skill 8. The bible records what the errors are.

Document every deliberate mess rule in the source so the site's hints and any future answer key can reference it.

### Filename and path safety (hard rules; the build fails on violation)

A previous test broke the download because of punctuation in filenames. Assume every learner is on Windows and extracting into `Downloads`.

- Filenames and folder names use only `A-Z a-z 0-9`, space, hyphen, underscore, full stop, and round brackets. No apostrophes, ampersands, commas, hashes, percent signs, plus signs, or any character from `< > : " / \ | ? *`. ASCII only: no accented letters, no emoji, even where a clan name would want them.
- No leading or trailing spaces or full stops. No consecutive full stops. No double spaces.
- No two names in the same folder that differ only by case (Windows is case-insensitive).
- Every full path inside the zip stays under 180 characters, so it survives extraction into a deep `Downloads` folder on a 260-character system. Deep nesting for the mess is fine; shorten the names to pay for it.
- Each file has exactly one extension, lower-case, from the shipped set: `docx xlsx pptx pdf txt csv vtt eml md png jpg`. Lock-file litter (`~$name.docx`) is allowed as the one exception to the character set, and only if it is proven to round-trip through the zip, the explorer URL and a Windows extraction in phase 1. Otherwise drop it.
- Any Windows reserved name (`CON`, `PRN`, `AUX`, `NUL`, `COM1` to `COM9`, `LPT1` to `LPT9`) is forbidden as a filename stem.
- The build runs a filename linter over the whole output tree and over the manifest before zipping. The explorer URL-encodes paths and is tested with every character in the permitted set.

### Architecture

Mirror how the rest of the repo works: config-driven, reproducible, Python.

```
hub/                               # new top-level folder
  README.md                        # how it works, how to add a document
  bible/                           # OUT OF WORLD, never shipped
    cast.yaml                      # characters, contact details
    sites.yaml                     # sites, libraries, folder skeletons
    storylines.md                  # threads and which files carry them
    mess_rules.yaml                # the deliberate chaos, declared
    classification.yaml            # label scheme and where labels are wrong on purpose
    planted_errors.yaml            # what is wrong in the in-world AI-generated files
    off_brand.yaml                 # which files break the brand guide, and how
    hand_supplied.yaml             # files the build copies but never regenerates (brand assets)
  source/                          # one authored file per estate document
    <site>/<library>/<path>/<name>.md     # YAML front-matter + body
  build/
    build_estate.py                # source -> real files in a tree + manifest.json
    renderers/                     # md->docx, table->xlsx, outline->pptx, transcript->vtt/docx, mail->eml, pdf
    make_mess.py                   # applies mess_rules deterministically (seeded)
    lint_filenames.py              # the safety rules above
    make_zips.py                   # whole-estate, per-site, per-library zips, each with README.txt
  output/                          # generated; gitignored except what ships
scripts/
  make_hub_extracts.py             # dataset -> Planning & Insight extracts and template values (see Data linkage)
docs/hub/                          # the public explorer (GitHub Pages)
  index.html                       # SharePoint-style explorer, reads manifest.json
  guide.html                       # Learner Guide, the fourth-wall page
  hub.js, hub.css
  data/manifest.json
  downloads/*.zip
```

**Source document front-matter** carries everything the estate needs: `title`, `author` (cast id), `modified`, `created`, `label`, `format` (docx/xlsx/pptx/pdf/txt/vtt/eml/csv), `site`, `library`, `path`, `lineage` (which document this is a version or copy of, and whether it is the true latest), `storyline` tags, `sensitive: true/false` (ground truth, independent of the label), `skills` it serves, and `mess` overrides. The body is Markdown (or a small DSL for spreadsheets and transcripts) that the renderer turns into the real file.

**Why authored, not generated:** minutes, policies and transcripts have to read right, and procedural text won't. Write them. Use templating only for the boring volume (meeting series, routine returns, repeated forms), for anything that quotes a number from the dataset, and for the mess.

**Transcripts** are the single most important content type for skill 1. Write at least six, varied: a short clean one; a sprawling 90-minute one with three tangents; a formal committee with papers; a panel with a reserved item that should not appear in open minutes; a one-to-one that strays into personal territory; a stand-up with heavy dwarvish and elvish name mangling. Pair each with the house-style minutes template and, for one or two, the actual minutes someone else produced so learners can compare.

**Data linkage** (skill 2) is part of the pipeline, not a one-off:

- `scripts/make_hub_extracts.py` reads `data/relational/` and writes two things into `hub/source/`: (a) the Planning & Insight data files (small extracts of a few thousand rows, summary workbooks, a programme-level awarding gap table, an NSS summary for the dipping department), and (b) a `values.yaml` of named figures (`he_good_degree_gap_1052`, `nursing_nss_overall_1052`, and so on) that in-world documents reference as template variables.
- `build_relational_outputs.py` calls it at the end, so running the pipeline with a new seed regenerates the Planning folder and every number quoted anywhere in the estate. Nothing in a source document hard-codes a figure that comes from the data.
- The "wrong numbers" in the Dean's draft are derived wrong (last year's figure, a transposed digit, the faculty figure used as the departmental one), so they stay wrong in the right way after a reseed.
- A "Data sources" note in Planning & Insight points to the Stonegrove download page. Do not copy the full dataset into the estate.

**The explorer** (`docs/hub/`): static, no backend, works from `manifest.json`. SharePoint-familiar without cloning any trademarked chrome: suite bar, left nav of sites, breadcrumb, document-library table (Name, Modified, Modified By, Label, Size), folder navigation, search across names, in-page preview for text-like files (the docx source rendered from Markdown, txt, vtt, csv), download buttons at estate, site, library and current-folder level. Prebuild zips for estate and site; building folder zips client-side with JSZip from the manifest is acceptable if prebuilding every folder is too heavy. Match the look of the existing Stonegrove site enough to feel like one family (fonts and palette from `docs/style.css`) while reading unmistakably as an intranet. Mobile-usable rather than mobile-first; this is a desktop activity.

**Hints overlay:** a toggle in the explorer. Off: pure intranet. On: each site, library and some folders gain a short callout saying what this team is wrestling with, what you might try here, and which skill it exercises, plus a "what's going on" panel per storyline. This is the only place the fourth wall breaks inside the explorer.

**Learner Guide** (`docs/hub/guide.html`): the fourth-wall page proper. What this is; each skill with two or three suggested exercises of increasing difficulty, where in the estate to start, what good looks like, and a reflective question; the governance documents as the ruleset; a plain note that every person, case and number is synthetic. Link from the main site nav alongside Explore, Prospectus and Download. Same house style as the rest of `docs/`.

### Constraints and conventions

- British English throughout, in-world and on the site. The in-world register is UK HE: programme, module, exam board, Senate, professional services.
- "Species", never "race". No real people, institutions, products or place names. Fictional tool names on the approved list.
- **Writing style, in-world and on the site:** no em dashes anywhere; use commas, colons, brackets or a full stop. Avoid the stock AI tells: "not X but Y" contrasts, tidy one-line closers, forced triads, "delve", "robust", "leverage", "streamline", "landscape", "journey", "crucial", "it's worth noting". Characters can be verbose or pompous in a human way, which is a different thing. Run a slop pass over every authored document before it ships.
- Everything reproducible: `python hub/build/build_estate.py --seed 42` regenerates the identical estate. Document the commands in `CLAUDE.md` alongside the pipeline commands.
- Pages limits: keep the shipped `docs/hub/` well under 100 MB; no single file over 25 MB.
- Repo is public but proprietary-licensed (see `LICENSE`). Downloads carry a short `README.txt` at the root of each zip saying what this is, that it is synthetic, and the licence.
- Use libraries already in `requirements.txt` where possible; add `python-docx`, `openpyxl`, `python-pptx`, `reportlab` (or similar) only as needed and pin them.
- Update the Release & Maintenance Checklist in `CLAUDE.md` with the hub's own steps (pipeline run regenerates extracts, then rebuild estate, lint, rebuild zips, regenerate manifest, check download page).

### Out of scope (say no to these unless asked)

- Automatic version history, check-in and check-out, permissions or sharing simulation. A static `Label` column is as far as access control goes.
- Authentication, a backend, search beyond filenames.
- Multiple faculties. One, done properly.
- Exposing any of the dataset's hidden mechanics (personality, motivation, stressors) in the estate. See the output-realism principle in the project memory.

### Phasing (one PR per phase; nothing ships until phase 1 is browsable end to end)

0. **Bible.** Cast with contact details, sites, storylines, label scheme, mess rules, planted errors, source-file schema, filename rules. Short design note in `hub/README.md`. Stop and review with Rosie before writing documents.
1. **Vertical slice.** Governance site complete (all policies), one faculty library, one transcript, `make_hub_extracts.py` wired into the pipeline, the build script producing real files and a manifest, the filename linter, the explorer rendering it, whole-estate download working and tested by extracting on Windows. Deployed to Pages with no link to it yet.
2. **Breadth.** Remaining sites and libraries, all transcripts, storylines threaded through, the additional-skill seeds, the Communications site with draft brand guide and placeholder assets, off-brand material planted.
3. **Mess and sensitivity pass.** `make_mess.py`, the StoneShare migration library, sensitive seeding with ground truth recorded, planted errors in the in-world AI files.
4. **Fourth wall.** Learner Guide, hints overlay, per-site, per-library and per-folder downloads, nav link from the main site, `CLAUDE.md` and README updates, slop pass over everything, QA against every skill by attempting each exercise with an AI tool and fixing what doesn't work.

Start with phase 0 and present the bible for review before writing a single in-world document.

Learner Guide

The Stonegrove Hub is the intranet of a university that does not exist. Every person, student, applicant, case, figure and document in it is synthetic. That is the point: you can practise using AI on realistic, messy institutional documents, including ones that look sensitive, with nothing real at risk.

Work in progress. All eight sites now have content and all the meeting transcripts are in. Still to come: the deliberate mess (duplicate versions, junk folders, the migrated StoneShare drive) and the full exercise set on this page.

How to use it

Browse the Hub as a member of staff would. Turn Hints on in the top bar to see what each team is wrestling with and what you might try there; turn them off for the plain intranet. Download everything, a site, a library or a folder, and point your AI tool at the files. The rules the University has set itself are in Governance and Secretariat, Policies and Procedures: start with the Data Classification Scheme, the AI Acceptable Use Policy and Can I use AI for this.

The skills

1. Meeting transcripts into minutes and actions

Take an auto-transcript and produce minutes in the house style, with actions, owners and dates. Then compare with the minutes the clerk actually produced.

Start: Governance and Secretariat, Senate, 4 December 1052. The Committee Handbook and the Minutes template are the house style.

2. Data analysis

Use the Planning and Insight extracts to answer the questions the Dean has been asked, and check the figures already circulating.

Start: Planning and Insight, Data Extracts. The full dataset is on the main Stonegrove site.

3. Project management, towards a bespoke app

Scope a small system from requirements that exist only as complaints, letters and a rejected form.

Start: Programme Support Team, Placements, then Provider Correspondence and the Digital Services request folder.

4. Folder organisation

Work out what a chaotic library should look like, write the rules, de-duplicate, decide what is current.

Coming with the StoneShare Migration library (phase 3). The Document Naming Convention and Records Retention Schedule are in Governance; the Deans Office library is a smaller warm-up.

5. AI guardrails

Instruct an AI to work across a folder without touching Confidential material. Then check whether it did, using the labels and, separately, the content.

Start: Faculty of Health and Education, Teaching Nursing and Care, Year 2. Read the Data Classification Scheme first.

6. Briefing a busy reader

Turn a long pack into a short brief that keeps what matters and flags what is contested.

Start: Governance and Secretariat, Council.

7. Extracting structure from unstructured documents

Thirty letters into one clean table.

Start: Programme Support Team, Provider Correspondence. Thirty-three letters, emails and agreements.

8. Checking the AI's work

Find the errors in documents someone else generated with a tool and did not check.

Start: Planning and Insight, Working (two files marked auto-generated) and the Deans Office FMG folder (AI draft minutes beside the transcript).

9. Reconciling versions

Five files called FINAL. Which is current and what changed?

Start: Governance and Secretariat, Policies and Procedures, Superseded, and the approved AI policy.

10. Translating institutional voice

Dense regulation into plain guidance; a difficult email in the right register.

Start: the Information Governance incident file; the Nursing and Care handbooks.

11. Following brand guidelines consistently

Give an AI the brand guide and see whether it holds the line across a set of materials, then whether it can fix the off-brand ones.

Start: Communications, Brand. Then the Brand Compliance Log, which tells you where to look.

A note on the sensitive-looking material

Some documents in the Hub read like real support plans, case notes and applicant records. They are fabricated, about fictional elves and dwarves, and written with realistic texture precisely so that the guardrails exercise means something. Treat them as you would the real thing while you practise, and remember that they are not.

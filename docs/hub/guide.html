Learner Guide

The Stonegrove Hub is the intranet of a university that does not exist. Every person, student, applicant, case, figure and document in it is synthetic. That is the point: you can practise using AI on realistic, messy institutional documents, including ones that look sensitive, with nothing real at risk.

How to use this. Browse the Hub as a member of staff would. Turn Hints on in the top bar to see what each team is wrestling with. Download everything, one site, one library or one folder, and point whatever AI tool you use at the files. The exercises below tell you where to start and what good looks like. Nothing inside the downloads breaks the fourth wall; this page and the hints are the only places that do.

Before you start

You need an AI tool that can read documents: a chat assistant you can upload files to, an assistant built into your office suite, or a coding assistant that can read a folder. The Hub does not mind which. The in-world University has an approved tool called Cloudi; that is part of the fiction, and your tool stands in for it.

Download what you need from the Hub: the Download everything button on the home page (about 18 MB), or the download button on any site, library or folder. The zip unpacks to a folder called stonegrove-hub with one folder per site. Files are real Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, text, transcript (.vtt) and email (.eml) files.

A word on what you will find. The estate is deliberately untidy: seven files called FINAL, folders called New folder, a drive that was migrated without anyone looking at it, lock files and temp files, and documents saved in the wrong place. Some documents are labelled Confidential; some should be and are not. That is the exercise, not a mistake in the exercise.

1. Meeting transcripts into minutes and actions

The skill

Take a long, messy auto-transcript and produce minutes in the house style, with an action list that has one named owner and a date for every action. Transcripts contain cross-talk, tangents, mis-transcribed names and decisions that are agreed and then re-agreed differently. The minutes should contain none of that and all of what was decided.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up The placements handover meeting, 27 March 1053. Short and clean. Produce minutes and an action table from the transcript, then compare with the notes Hesta wrote. What did she leave out, and was she right to?
  2. Standard Senate, 4 December 1052, item 7: the AI policy debate. Produce a minute of item 7 in the Committee Handbook style (standard verbs in capitals, contributions unattributed except where dissent is recorded). Compare with minute 24.7 in Gudrik's approved minutes. His version is one paragraph; yours probably is not.
  3. Hard The Faculty Management Group, 19 March 1053: 90 minutes on the agenda, three tangents, a dog, and a decision about the Council paper that nobody quite made. Produce minutes. Then read the file called AI minutes DRAFT in the same folder, which someone produced with a tool and did not check, and list everything it got wrong.
  4. Hard The Education NSS action group. Every name is mangled by the transcription. Work out who is who from the module team contacts list and the context, and produce minutes with the right names.

What good looks like

Past tense, third person, numbered items, the decision stated with the house verbs, discussion summarised in two or three sentences, every action with an owner who is a named post-holder and a date that is a date. Reserved items minuted separately or not at all. Nothing in the minutes that is not in the transcript.

Think about

The OT revalidation panel transcript contains a reserved item about a named member of staff. If you asked a tool to minute it, would you have told the tool which part to leave out? Would it have obeyed?

2. Data analysis

The skill

Answer a question with data, and know what the data cannot answer. The Hub links to the Stonegrove dataset through Planning and Insight, Data Extracts: small cuts for this Faculty. The full dataset is on the main site if you want the whole University.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up From the good honours extract, produce the five-year table of the Elf to Dwarf gap for the Faculty. Check it against the Awarding Gap Annual Report 1051-52 in Planning's Reports library. They should agree; if they do not, work out why.
  2. Standard The Dean's draft Council paper in Deans Office quotes three figures. Check each against the extracts. Write the two-paragraph correction Quorin would send. Then read the one he did send, in Planning, Data Requests.
  3. Standard NSS 1052: which programmes actually fell on Assessment and Feedback? Does the Department of Education have NSS results at all? Compare your answer with the deep dive in Reports and the auto-generated summary in Working.
  4. Hard Admissions have three explanations for a conversion dip. Using the weekly dashboard export in Admissions, Reports, decide how much of the headline gap is timing. Write the one paragraph that settles the argument.

What good looks like

Every figure with its denominator and its source. The in-world year convention understood (graduating year 1052 means academic year 1051-52). Programme-level figures suppressed or caveated where numbers are small. A clear statement of what the data cannot tell you. Quorin's "How to read our reports" in Reports / 1050-51 is the standard.

Think about

An AI will happily compute a gap for a programme with eleven graduates. Who is responsible when it is quoted?

3. Project management, towards a bespoke app

The skill

Scope a small system from requirements that exist only as complaints, letters, a spreadsheet and a rejected form. Nobody in the Programme Support Team has written a requirements document. Everything a requirements document needs is nonetheless in their folders.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up Read Dagna's "Things that go wrong with placements" and Halden's handover document. Turn them into a numbered list of testable requirements in the form the Digital Services request form asks for ("The system must ...").
  2. Standard Open the placement allocation workbook (the one that says use this one). Describe the current process as a numbered sequence of steps with an owner for each, as section 7 of the request form requires. Use the Issues tab and Hesta's 1050 process document (in StoneShare Migration / Hesta stuff) to find the steps nobody wrote down.
  3. Hard Complete the Digital Services project request form and the Governance business case template for a placements system, including the data section: what the system must hold, its classification, and what it must not hold (read the Advice Given log in Governance for Hesta's question about the OH column). Then read Fenn's 1051 decline and check your submission against each of his four reasons.
  4. Stretch Build a prototype. A spreadsheet with validation, a small database, a form and a lookup: whatever your tool can make. It must answer Halden's Friday-afternoon question (who is affected, who has space, who is cleared, who needs what) in under a minute.

What good looks like

Requirements that someone could test. A process that someone else could follow. A data section that holds less than the spreadsheet does now. Halden's requirement written in his words.

4. Folder organisation: imposing structure and rules

The skill

Take a library that is chaos and work out what it should be: what to keep, what to delete, where things go, what they should be called. The StoneShare Migration library is the old network drive, copied across in one go. The rules are the Naming Convention and the Retention Schedule in Governance. Liora's index in the To sort folder is a map made by someone who asked the same question.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up List every file in StoneShare Migration with: what it is, roughly when it is from, and which line of the Retention Schedule applies. Mark the ones that are past their retention date.
  2. Standard Propose a target structure: which library each surviving document moves to, with its new name under the convention. Write the rules you applied, so that someone else could apply them to the next drive.
  3. Standard The Placements / Allocation folder in the Programme Support Team has seven versions of one workbook. Decide which is current (the handover transcript and Hesta's notes are evidence), name it under the convention, and say what happens to the other six.
  4. Hard Find the three things in StoneShare Migration that should not be on a shared drive at all, say which rule each breaks, and write the short email to each owner. Then ask: would an AI that read only file names have found them?

What good looks like

Rules first, then the application of the rules, then the exceptions with reasons. Nothing deleted that the schedule says keep; nothing kept that the schedule says destroy. Hesta's folder recognised for what it is.

5. AI guardrails

The skill

Instruct an AI to work across a set of files without touching Confidential material, and then check whether it did. The Data Classification Scheme says Confidential material must not be entered into any tool. The estate contains Confidential material that is correctly labelled, Confidential material labelled Internal, and Confidential material with no label at all. A tool that trusts the labels will read things it should not. A tool that reads the content to decide may do better, and has by then read the content.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up Download Teaching Nursing and Care. Ask your tool to summarise every document in the library, skipping anything Confidential. Then look at what it summarised. Was anything in Year 2 / Staff only something a teaching team should not have had?
  2. Standard Write an instruction for your tool (a system prompt, a rule, a checklist) that would have stopped it. Test it on the same library. Then test it on StoneShare Migration, where nothing has a label.
  3. Standard Before using any tool, list by hand every file in the Faculty and Quality sites that you believe is Confidential whatever its label says. Then compare your list with what your tool treated as Confidential.
  4. Hard The incident file in Governance (INC-0031) says a leaked support plan was removed from a teaching folder. Find out whether it was. Then write the email the Information Governance Manager should send, and the line the Data Breach Procedure needed.

What good looks like

A written rule that distinguishes label from content and says what to do when they disagree. A check that was done, not assumed. An honest account of what the tool read before it decided not to.

Think about

The one-page adjustments summary beside the leaked plan names the same student and is correctly labelled Internal by the University's own procedure. Would your rule have blocked it too? Should it?

6. Briefing a busy reader

The skill

Turn a long pack into a short brief that keeps what matters and flags what is contested. The Chair of Council asked for two pages and got a 212-page pack. The Dean writes "can someone tell me what this actually says". The Council folders and the Quality revalidation folder are the material.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up One page for the Dean on the OT revalidation: outcome, conditions, deadlines, what he needs to do. Sources: the panel report, the minutes, and Caerel's response draft. Do not use the auto-generated summary in Planning's Working folder (or do, and then check it).
  2. Standard Write the two-page Council paper the Chair asked for: the figures (from Planning, not from the Dean's draft), what the Faculty will do, how it will know. Use the committee paper template. Keep the cover sheet to 150 words.
  3. Hard Brief a new Head of Communications in one page on where the brand stands eighteen months after launch, from the Compliance Log, the Requests folder and Rhialla's lessons learned. Say what you would do first.

What good looks like

The reader can make the decision from your page alone. Contested points are marked as contested, with both sides in a sentence each. Every figure traceable. No adjectives about the quality of the work.

7. Extracting structure from unstructured documents

The skill

Thirty-three letters, emails and agreements in Provider Correspondence, each with a contact, a capacity, conditions and dates buried in prose, in four different formats. Produce one clean table.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up Table with columns: provider, contact, phone, email, places, pathway restrictions, pre-start requirements, year of letter. One row per letter.
  2. Standard Reconcile to one row per provider for 1052-53. Some providers wrote twice with different numbers; Greywater Vale Infirmary's Outpatients places count within the Infirmary's total of twelve. Say how you resolved each conflict.
  3. Hard Check every row against its source letter, not a sample. Record your error rate. Then compare your table with Hesta's 1050 provider register in StoneShare Migration / Hesta stuff: what did the shape of hers get right?

What good looks like

A table Liora could hand to Halden's cover. Conflicts resolved explicitly, not silently. The checking done and the error rate stated. Liora asked Elenna whether she was allowed to use a tool on these letters before she did; her answer is in the Advice Given log.

8. Checking the AI's work

The skill

Several documents in the estate were produced with a tool and never checked. They read well. They are wrong in specific ways: a figure doubled, an action given to the wrong person, a date a month out, a quotation that appears in no source, a caveat dropped. Your job is verification, not generation.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up NSS 1052 pack summary (auto-generated) against the Faculty NSS deep dive in Reports. Find at least four errors.
  2. Standard External examiner reports OT summary (auto-generated) against the three reports in Quality, External Examiners. Find the inversion, the wrong institution, the invented quotation and the wrong count.
  3. Hard FMG 19 March AI minutes DRAFT against the transcript in the same folder. One attendee was not there. One action is assigned to someone never named. One tangent became a decision. One twenty-minute item vanished. One date is invented. Find all five, then write Elowen the note she needs.
  4. Hard The Dean's draft Council paper was written by a person, not a tool, and has three errors a careful reader can catch from the extracts. Is checking human work any different?

What good looks like

Each error stated as: what the document says, what the source says, where in the source. No error reported that you have not confirmed. A one-line verdict on whether the document can be fixed or must be redone.

9. Reconciling versions

The skill

Several files with the same name and different suffixes. Which is current, what changed between them, and which changes were deliberate. File dates lie: some FINALs are older than their DRAFTs.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up Governance, Policies and Procedures, Superseded: two AI policy drafts and the approved policy in Current. Table the differences between A, B and 1.0. Which draft did Senate's debate move the policy towards?
  2. Standard The seven placement allocation workbooks. Which is the true latest? The transcript of 27 March tells you what the team believes; the files tell you what is true. Are they the same?
  3. Standard Five Education action plans with no version numbers. Order them by content, not by date. Which is final, and what does "final" say about the earlier versions?
  4. Hard The OT self-evaluation document in three versions and the procedure for sharing adjustments in two. For each, write the one-paragraph change log the author should have written.

What good looks like

A verdict on the current version with the evidence for it. A list of what changed, in order. A recommendation for what to do with the rest (the Naming Convention has a view). Awareness of where the file dates mislead.

10. Translating institutional voice

The skill

Rewrite dense regulation as plain guidance, or draft a difficult email in the right register, without changing what it means. The regulations in Quality are written to be quoted. The incident file and the Requests folder in Communications are full of emails that had to be written carefully.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up Rewrite Part C (Extenuating Circumstances) as a one-page student guide. Read the two existing student guides in the Guidance folder for the register, and Caerel's draft EC guide for what not to do with a worked example.
  2. Standard Rewrite the Admissions offer letter template so that it is warm, on brand (Brand Guide section 6), and still makes Mira happy. Her tone-of-voice note tells you what she is afraid of losing.
  3. Standard Draft the email the Head of Student Wellbeing should send to the Programme Leader when she discovers the leaked file is still in the teaching folder six weeks after it was reported removed. Firm, not cruel, with the procedure cited.
  4. Hard Write Sorell's reply to Hrogar's second letterhead request from scratch, then compare with the reply in the Declined folder. What did the real reply offer that a refusal does not?

What good looks like

Meaning preserved exactly; register changed completely. Regulation paragraph numbers kept where a reader might need them. Emails the recipient could act on and would not forward in anger.

11. Following brand guidelines consistently

The skill

Give an AI the Brand Guide and ask it to produce or fix a run of material, then check whether it held the line across all of it: name, retired names, logo rules, palette, type, voice, exclamation marks, hashtag. The Brand Compliance Log lists 24 sightings of where people did not.

Exercises

  1. Warm-up With the Brand Guide loaded, produce: an open day flyer, a programme leaflet for Education, a week of social posts, and an email signature. Then audit your own output against the guide's section 8. Count the breaches.
  2. Standard Take Javin's March socials schedule and rewrite every post Sorell marked N so that it passes. Keep what he was trying to say.
  3. Standard Take Jaskel's Education leaflet and his 42-slide deck. Produce on-brand versions that keep what the students like about them. Sorell's reply in the Requests folder says what she offered; was it enough?
  4. Hard Work through the Compliance Log. For each pending or declined sighting, find the file in the estate, say which rule it breaks, and produce the fix or the lock-up Sorell would. Then write the paragraph for Rhialla's lessons learned that she did not write: how a guide becomes a decision.

What good looks like

Consistency across a set, not just within one piece. Retired names caught everywhere, including footers. One typeface pair. No gradient, no sub-logo, no second exclamation mark. The warmth Admissions wanted, kept.

For facilitators

The Hub is built from authored source documents and a declared set of mess rules, so the ground truth is always known. data/manifest.json behind the explorer records, for every file, its real classification (sensitive), whether it is a planted inconsistency, a planted error or an off-brand item, and, for every duplicate or stale version, which file it was derived from and by which rule. The project repository holds the bible that lists every planted item with what is wrong about it. Learners should not read it. You may.

The estate regenerates from the Stonegrove dataset, so figures change when the data does; the exercises are written against relationships (which report is current, which figure is stale) rather than specific numbers.

A note on the sensitive-looking material

Some documents read like real support plans, case notes, fitness to practise files and applicant records. They are fabricated, about fictional elves and dwarves, and written with realistic texture precisely so that the guardrails exercise means something. Treat them as you would the real thing while you practise, and remember that they are not.

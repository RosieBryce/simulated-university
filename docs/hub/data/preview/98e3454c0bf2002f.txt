## Aims

To educate occupational therapists who understand occupation as the means and the end of therapy; who can assess, plan and evaluate interventions with people whose lives have been disrupted by illness, injury or circumstance; and who can work across settings, from acute to community, with the person's own goals at the centre.

## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the relationship between occupation, health and wellbeing.
2. Assess occupational performance using recognised tools and clinical reasoning.
3. Plan, deliver and evaluate interventions in partnership with the person.
4. Work within the multidisciplinary team and across agencies.
5. Practise within the professional code, the law and the evidence.
6. Reflect critically on their own practice.
7. Undertake and appraise research relevant to practice.

## Structure

| Year | Credits | Placement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 120 across six modules: foundations of occupation, anatomy and physiology, psychology for therapy, introduction to practice, professional studies, research methods 1 | 4 weeks |
| 2 | 120 across six modules, each with two assessment points (the external examiner has recommended reducing this; see the self-evaluation document) | 8 weeks and 6 weeks |
| 3 | 120: dissertation (40), advanced practice (40), two option modules, final placement | 10 weeks |

## Assessment

Coursework, examinations, practical and viva assessments, placement records and the dissertation. Year 2 currently carries twelve assessment points. Degree classification is by the University's standard weighting (Year 2 one third, Year 3 two thirds).

## Admission

Standard University entry requirements, a health declaration, enhanced vetting and interview.

## Fitness to practise

Students are subject to the University's Fitness to Practise Procedure throughout.
